## D-016 (2026-10-06): download throughput is limited per connection, not by our link or the rate limit
First full run (4 workers, 1 file per second allowed): about 12 files and 9 MB per minute, 4 notices per minute, which would take about 20 hours for 4,800 notices. Cause: `curl` timings on three real attachments gave **25 to 36 KB/s per connection** (first byte after 1.6 to 2.7 s; 588 KB took 19.7 s) while the same machine downloads 10 MB from a CDN at 7 MB/s. SAM.gov's storage serves each connection slowly; a 50 MB file would occupy a worker for about 28 minutes. Changes (configs/acquisition.toml): **24 workers** (the shared 1-request-per-second limit still applies to the start of each file) and **`max_seconds_per_file = 180`** (a file that takes longer is skipped with reason `too_slow` and listed in the journal, never silently dropped); leftover `.tmp-*` files of a killed run are removed at start; a replayed notice no longer duplicates its document rows. The size limit stays at 50 MB as in the plan. Expected effect: about 6x the throughput; the skipped `too_slow` files are counted at Checkpoint 1 so the cost of the cap is visible.

## D-019 (2026-10-06): no human annotators; labels come from a panel of local models, anchored by non-LLM truth
**Context.** The owner has no professional annotators, and wants the work to run autonomously. Plan 9.4 to 9.6 assumed a human gold set.
**Decision.** (1) *Reference labels* = an adjudicated panel: qwen3.5:9b (two prompt variants), qwen3.5:4b and the rules baseline B1 vote on every mention; temperature 0, JSON validated against the schema, candidate ids supplied (a model never writes a clause number). Tiers: unanimous (accepted), majority (accepted, weighted lower), split (excluded from scoring, sent to one paid pass if budget allows). (2) *Three evaluation slices, always reported separately*: A. objective truth with no LLM in it: documents whose checkbox state is a real form widget or typed box (deterministic selected / not selected), B. synthetic documents rendered from the registry with exact labels, C. panel-adjudicated real documents. Claims about "accuracy" are made only for A and B; C is called agreement with a panel. (3) *Reliability* replaces human IAA: Cohen's kappa between panel members, self-consistency across prompt variants, and the share of split mentions. (4) A 30-item audit pack for the owner (CTO, not a procurement specialist) is optional and never blocks a phase. (5) Paid pass: owner budget USD 10 to 15 in total, local first; spend logged in `results/spend.json`; no single run over USD 5 without a printed dry-run estimate.
**Consequences.** Test-set numbers measure agreement with the panel and the objective slices, not expert accuracy; the paper and cards say so. If the panel is wrong in a correlated way (same model family), slices A and B are what expose it.
**Also:** DoD DPAP page fetched by hand (host certificate not trusted by this machine: DoD CA; public data, verification disabled for that host only, results parsed as data); the DoD Part 52 deviation 2026-O0038 (effective 2026-02-01) points to the acquisition.gov RFO Part 52 text already in the registry; 47 DFARS deviation memos parsed into `data/processed/deviations_dod.parquet` (18 are revisions with no stated effective date). Only fedproc-ledger is subject to the no-trailer rule; the other repos are left as they are (owner, 2026-10-06).

## D-020 (2026-10-07): first results of the panel and the feature model; what they do and do not show
**Panel (pilot, 20 documents, 1,352 mentions).** The three-LLM panel was unreliable: 49% of mentions split, pairwise kappa 0.15 to 0.46. A bug in `build_items` (pandas NaN is truthy) put "[cited_date: nan]" into most prompts; fixed with a test, all votes re-run. With clean prompts, on slice A (211 checklist box items, objective), qwen3.5:9b scores 73.9% (variant B), 85.3% (C, few-shot) and 87.7% (D, few-shot plus rules); a deterministic glyph rule scores 100%. Decision: box-defined items are decided by rule; LLMs are not used for them.
**Agent-labelled audit sets** (annotator: the coding agent, not a human; `results/audit_claude_pilot.json` 120 items, `results/audit_claude_round2.json` 300 items, UNCLEAR excluded: 108 and 250 usable). On the unbiased round-2 sample (non-box mentions, 60 documents): B1 role agreement 33%, binding agreement 64%; 9b-C 46% / 75%.
**B1 weakness found.** B1 labels most table rows and wrapped list entries "NARRATIVE" (610 of 1,352 pilot mentions; only 3 of 45 audited ones were narrative) and finds FULL_TEXT on 7. The section detector also never records the checklist clause (`clause` is None on checklist lines) and labels SF 1449 block 27 lines as checklist. So B1 under-detects binding clauses: ledger precision 0.99 but recall 0.59 against the panel (pilot, 20 documents).
**Feature model (scripts/cv_features.py, 5-fold CV grouped by document, 1,396 labelled mentions of which 358 non-box).** On the 358 non-box mentions: B1 35.2% role / 62.6% binding; 9b-C vote 50.0% / 76.3%; logistic regression on structural features 73.7% / 88.5% (macro-F1 0.485; rare roles are weak); adding B1 or the vote as features did not help (69.6 to 71.8%). Box items: 100% for features and B1.
**Caveats (must travel with these numbers).** (1) Labels come from one agent reading the contexts, so errors are correlated with how the features were designed; I read the same items when choosing features. (2) Groups are documents, not solicitations or agency templates; templated documents (VA, DHS, USDA forms) can leak across folds, so the numbers are optimistic. (3) Labels are mention-level, not document ledgers; the primary metric (binding-set F1) is not yet measured on the learned model. (4) 358 hard items is small; intervals are wide (about plus or minus 5 points).
**Next.** Template-aware grouping (agency + layout signature), a fine-tuned encoder on the same labels, more labels where models disagree, and a ledger-level evaluation on the pilot documents.
**D-020 addendum, stricter grouping (scripts/cv_groups.py, 358 non-box mentions scored, 5-fold).** Logistic regression, role / binding agreement: grouped by document 73.7 / 88.5 (features), 77.7 / 91.6 (features + char TF-IDF); by solicitation 72.1 / 88.0 and 76.8 / 91.1; leaving departments out (14 departments) 76.3 / 89.7 and 76.5 / 91.1 (solver changed to liblinear one-vs-rest for speed, so the department rows are not a pure grouping comparison). Reading: the gain over B1 (35 / 63) and the 9b vote (50 / 76) does not depend on seeing the same document or department in training. Still agent labels, still mention-level, still about 5 points of noise.

## D-021 (2026-10-07): no Qwen or other PRC-origin models in this project; OpenAI small models for judging, hard spend cap
**Why (owner):** this is a US federal-procurement project; Qwen (Alibaba) is not to be used for its labelling, judging or baselines. The owner's local Ollama and its Qwen models stay as they are (they serve other work and are not touched by this repo).

**Replacement:** `fl label run|lab|audit --backend openai` with a model such as gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4.1-nano or gpt-4.1-mini (`label/openai_chat.py`). Key in `.env` as OPENAI_API_KEY (never printed or logged). Use cases, in order: judge only the items the feature model is least sure of; score the audit sets as an independent labeller; silver labels for rare roles.
